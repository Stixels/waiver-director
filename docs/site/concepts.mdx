title: 'Key concepts'
description: 'The terms Waiver Director uses throughout the app.'
---

## Workspace

A workspace is an isolated boundary for one waiver operator, such as a business or one location of a business. Every waiver, booking, submission, customer, and email belongs to exactly one workspace, and data is never shared between workspaces. One person can own several workspaces.

## Owner and Staff

Workspace members are either **Owners** or **Staff**. Staff handle day-to-day operations. Owners can also manage integrations, workspace identity, and deleting the workspace. See [Team and roles](/account/team-and-roles).

## Waiver template and published version

The waiver you edit is a draft. When you publish, Waiver Director saves a frozen **published waiver version**. Signers always sign a specific version, and that version never changes, even if you edit and republish later.

## Signed submission

A signed submission is the permanent record of someone signing a published version: their details, custom field answers, signature, any covered minors, and the booking details at the time of signing. Signed submissions cannot be edited.

## Customer and participant

A **customer** is a person your workspace recognizes, usually by email address, who may sign waivers and receive follow-ups. A **participant** is anyone who takes part in a booking, including people who never sign on their own, such as a **covered minor** listed on an adult's waiver.

## Booking

A booking is a scheduled experience imported from a booking provider like Bookeo. Waiver Director uses bookings to show who still needs to sign; it does not replace your booking system.

## Waiver gap

A booking has a waiver gap when its expected participant count is higher than the number of participants covered by signed submissions. Each submission covers the signer plus any minors on it. Canceled bookings are excluded from current gaps.

## Follow-up

A follow-up is an email created from a signed submission and sent after the experience, such as a thank-you, a feedback request, or a review request.
